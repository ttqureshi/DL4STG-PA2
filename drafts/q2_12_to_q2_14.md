## Q2.12

Model D is Model A’s forecaster with dilation 2 on both ConvLSTM layers. There is no attention. Each layer has 64 hidden channels, a 3×3 kernel, stride 1, dilation 2, and padding 2. The rollout is Model A’s. `pixel_unshuffle(4)` folds each frame. Layer 1 reads the folded frame. Layer 2 reads layer 1’s new hidden state at the same step. A 1×1 head, `pixel_shuffle(4)`, and a sigmoid paint the frame. The forward pass is nine steps. Steps 1–5 read the five real frames. Steps 6–9 read the network’s own previous prediction. The five returned frames are the outputs of steps 5–9. Hidden and cell states start at zero.

Model D’s one-step reach is 16 original pixels. Model A’s reach, from Q2.3, is 8. Sixteen is strictly larger than 8. The bound is a property of one update, so it is the same for an identity input, a mirror, and a rotation, and the same at 1×, 2×, and 3×.

The measurement is centre-to-centre on the original 64×64 frame. `pixel_unshuffle(4)` folds each 4×4 patch into channels and leaves a 16×16 grid. Neighbouring cells on that grid have centres 4 original pixels apart. The fold does not bring in any cell beyond the patch that is already sitting there, and the 4×4 patch width is not added. `pixel_shuffle` is the same regrouping in reverse, and the 1×1 head mixes channels at one cell. Neither moves information to another cell.

Both ConvLSTM layers use a 3×3 kernel, stride 1, dilation 2, and padding 2. A 3×3 kernel with dilation 1 reads offsets −1, 0, and +1, so it moves one folded cell. Dilation 2 spaces those same three taps by 2, so the offsets are −2, 0, and +2. One application therefore moves information by 2 folded cells. Two folded cells are 2 × 4 = 8 original pixels. Padding 2 keeps the map at 16×16. At the border the kernel is cut off by the padding; the longest path is the one in the interior. The gate nonlinearities and the cell update are pointwise, so they do not add distance.

Inside one step τ there are three paths from the sources named in Q2.3 to the prediction \(\hat{x}_{\tau+1}\).

The new folded input \(u_\tau\) is concatenated with \(h^{(1)}_{\tau-1}\) and read by layer 1’s dilated 3×3. That produces \(h^{(1)}_\tau\). Layer 2 reads \(h^{(1)}_\tau\), not the previous hidden state, concatenated with \(h^{(2)}_{\tau-1}\), through its own dilated 3×3. The 1×1 convolution and `pixel_shuffle` then paint the frame. Two dilated 3×3 kernels, and nothing else that moves spatially: 2 cells + 2 cells = 4 folded cells, and 4 × 4 = 16 original pixels.

\(h^{(1)}_{\tau-1}\) enters that same layer-1 kernel and then the layer-2 kernel. It also travels 4 folded cells, so it also reaches 16 original pixels.

\(h^{(2)}_{\tau-1}\) enters only the layer-2 kernel, then the 1×1 head. That is 2 folded cells, 8 original pixels.

The cell state does not open a longer path. \(c_{\tau-1}\) is multiplied, at the same cell, by the forget gate. Its value does not shift onto a neighbour. The recurrence still matters: the previous hidden states are inputs to the convolutions, so memory can move, but only as far as the convolutions applied in this step. Because layer 2 consumes the new \(h^{(1)}_\tau\), the new frame and layer 1’s previous state are stacked through both kernels. Layer 2’s previous state sees only one kernel.

The longest path is therefore 16 original pixels. One path that attains it is \(u_\tau \rightarrow\) layer-1 3×3 dilation 2 \(\rightarrow h^{(1)}_\tau \rightarrow\) layer-2 3×3 dilation 2 \(\rightarrow \hat{x}_{\tau+1}\).

Model A’s longest path is those same two kernels with dilation 1. Each moves 1 folded cell: 1 + 1 = 2 cells, and 2 × 4 = 8 original pixels. Dilation 2 changes only the spacing of the taps. Each kernel’s step goes from 1 cell to 2 cells, the two kernels still stack, and 4 × 4 = 16.

Dilation does not add weights. Each layer is still one 3×3 convolution with bias, four gates, and 64 hidden channels.

Layer 1 reads 16 folded channels plus 64 hidden channels and writes 4 × 64 gate channels:

\((16 + 64) \times (4 \times 64) \times 3 \times 3 + (4 \times 64) = 80 \times 256 \times 9 + 256 = 184{,}576\).

Layer 2 reads 64 + 64 channels:

\((64 + 64) \times 256 \times 9 + 256 = 128 \times 256 \times 9 + 256 = 295{,}168\).

The 1×1 head maps 64 channels to 16:

\(64 \times 16 + 16 = 1{,}040\).

Total: \(184{,}576 + 295{,}168 + 1{,}040 = 480{,}784\).

The cap is \(1.5 \times 480{,}784 = 721{,}176\). Model D’s 480,784 parameters sit under that cap. The count is `sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad)`. Model A, the same cell at dilation 1, also counts 480,784. Models B and C use that cell, so they count 480,784 as well.

**Mirror, Model A, 1×: appearance.** The Table 5 comparison is Model B against Model A under the mirror at 1×. Model B’s \(C_M\) is 0.00106 (0.00108, 0.00105), down from Model A’s 0.00183, and Model B’s \(E_M\) is 0.0224 (0.0227, 0.0221), in line with Model B’s own \(E(5)\) of 0.0223 on the original 1× clips. The architecture is unchanged, so a reach limit would still be there. The speed-up is unchanged too: Model B’s \(E(2)\) stays at 0.0446 (0.0444, 0.0448) at 2× and 0.0506 (0.0496, 0.0515) at 3×, next to Model A’s 0.0448 and 0.0520. What Model B added is the mirrored glyph at 1×, and that is the test that moves. Model C, which trains on the speed-up and never on the mirror, has \(E_M = 0.0269\) (0.0270, 0.0269) at 1×, worse than Model A’s 0.0227 and Model B’s 0.0224. Its \(C_M\) of \(9.30\times10^{-4}\) says the two orders agree more closely, while both sit farther from the mirrored future.

**Rotation, Model A, 1×: appearance.** The Table 5 comparison is Model B against Model A under the rotation at 1×. Model B’s \(C_R\) is 0.00132 (0.00126, 0.00139), down from Model A’s 0.00308, and Model B’s \(E_R\) is 0.0223 (0.0226, 0.0219), matching Model B’s \(E(5)\) of 0.0223 on the original 1× clips. Model A’s \(E_R\) was 0.0238. The same group augmentation that supplies the rotated glyph closes the larger of the two symmetry gaps, and the 2× and 3× speed-up errors stay with Model A, as quoted above. Model C’s \(E_R\) at 1× is 0.0279 (0.0279, 0.0278), above Model A’s 0.0238. Speed-up training does not restore the rotated forecast. Model D, reach 16 and the same speed schedule, has \(E_R = 0.0296\) (0.0292, 0.0299) at 1×. A longer kernel without the rotated glyph does not either.

**Speed-up, Model A, 2×: unfamiliar motion.** The Table 5 comparison is Model C against Model A and Model B at 2×, with Model D as the reach check. Model C has Model A’s 8-pixel reach and has seen 2× windows. Its \(E(2)\) at 2× is 0.0288 (0.0288, 0.0289), down from Model A’s 0.0448 (0.0449, 0.0448). Model B, same reach, no speed-up in training, is still at 0.0446 (0.0444, 0.0448). Holding the architecture fixed and changing the training motion removes the 2× failure. Widening the reach does not: Model D’s \(E(2)\) at 2× is 0.0293 (0.0289, 0.0297), level with Model C. A typical 2× step is 6.64 original pixels per frame, inside the 8-pixel path. The 2× error was a step the 1× sample had not shown, and the kernels could already carry it.

**Speed-up, Model A, 3×: unfamiliar motion, and then architectural reach.** Model C at 3× is better than Model A at 3×, and Model C at 3× is still worse than Model D at 3×. Model C’s \(E(2)\) at 3× is 0.0335 (0.0336, 0.0334). Model A’s is 0.0520 (0.0528, 0.0511). Model D’s is 0.0316 (0.0311, 0.0321). Both seeds of Model C sit below both seeds of Model A, and both sit above both seeds of Model D. Model B, same reach as A and C, no 3× training, remains at 0.0506 (0.0496, 0.0515).

The drop from Model A to Model C at 3× is unfamiliar motion. The reach is still 8 original pixels, the glyph is still the original one, and the new training signal is the fast step. Slower digits at 3×, and the part of a fast digit that lands inside 8 pixels, are steps the architecture can already represent once they appear in training. The stored means fall by 0.0185.

The remaining gap, Model C at 0.0335 against Model D at 0.0316, is architectural reach. The two models share the speed schedule. They differ by the dilation. A typical 3× step is 9.95 original pixels per frame, from three times the 3.32 median in Q2.1. That step is past Model C’s longest path of 8 and inside Model D’s longest path of 16. The stored means differ by 0.00185.

More training data cannot carry information farther than the architecture allows. A training window shows the loss a 9.95-pixel jump. The gradient can only change the weights on paths the forward pass already has. In Model C those paths stop at 8 original pixels: two dilation-1 kernels, 2 folded cells, 2 × 4. A cell that must paint the digit’s new position never receives the digit’s previous position when the two are more than 8 original pixels apart. Further 3× examples repeat that same graph. They do not insert the extra folded cell that dilation 2 inserts, and they do not turn Model C’s 2 + 2 cells into Model D’s 4. The mirror, the rotation, and the 1×, 2×, and 3× speed-ups all meet that same cutoff, because none of them adds a spatial edge.

Model B’s mean, 0.0223, and Model A’s mean, 0.0221, differ by \(2.88\times10^{-4}\) in the stored means. Each model’s seeds overlap the other’s range. On the original 1× clips the symmetry training did not move the error. It was worth it. Under the mirror at 1×, \(C_M\) falls from 0.00183 to 0.00106 and \(E_M\) stays at 0.0224. Under the rotation at 1×, \(C_R\) falls from 0.00308 to 0.00132 and \(E_R\) falls to 0.0223, level with the original \(E(5)\).

Model C’s \(E(5)\) at 1× on the identity is 0.0267 (0.0268, 0.0267). Both seeds sit above both of Model A’s seeds. The stored means differ by 0.00467. The same training is what cuts the speed-up error: \(E(2)\) at 2× falls from Model A’s 0.0448 to Model C’s 0.0288, a drop of 0.0160, and \(E(2)\) at 3× falls from 0.0520 to 0.0335, a drop of 0.0185. Both drops are larger than the 0.00467 rise on the original clips. The speed-up change was worth it for the 2× and 3× clips. On the original 1× clips it is a real loss, and Model C’s \(E(2)\) there rises from 0.0186 to 0.0230.

Model D’s \(E(5)\) at 1× on the identity is 0.0287 (0.0283, 0.0290), the highest of the four. Against Model C the stored means differ by 0.00193. Against Model A they differ by 0.00660. At 3×, Model D’s \(E(2)\) of 0.0316 is the lowest, 0.00185 under Model C. At 2×, Model D’s 0.0293 does not improve on Model C’s 0.0288. The dilation was worth it on the 3× speed-up, where the step of 9.95 original pixels exceeds the 8-pixel reach and Model D is the model that goes lower. It was not worth it on the original 1× clips. The median step there is 3.32 pixels per frame, inside Model A’s reach, and Model D pays the largest \(E(5)\) on those clips. Against Model C, that 1× cost of 0.00193 is the same size as the 3× gain of 0.00185.

## Q2.15

Built-in symmetry is exact. Learned symmetry leaves a gap.

\(G\) has eight elements, and each one acts linearly on a clip. For any \(h \in G\), right multiplication by \(h\) permutes \(G\), and \(h\) factors out of the average. Therefore \(\bar f(h(X)) = h(\bar f(X))\) for every underlying \(f\). \(M\) and \(R\) are in \(G\), so \(C_M\) and \(C_R\) are identically zero. The weights never enter. On the 1,000 test videos the measured gaps are the floating-point residual of that identity.

Its \(C_M\) is 0.00106 (0.00108, 0.00105) and its \(C_R\) is 0.00132 (0.00126, 0.00139). \(E_M\) is 0.0224 (0.0227, 0.0221) and \(E_R\) is 0.0223 (0.0226, 0.0219), level with its own \(E(5)\) of 0.0223 (0.0226, 0.0221). Learned D+B uses dilation 2, the same group, and speeds \(k \in \{1, 2, 3\}\). Its \(C_M\) is \(7.53\times10^{-4}\) (\(7.49\times10^{-4}\), \(7.57\times10^{-4}\)) and its \(C_R\) is \(9.89\times10^{-4}\) (\(9.57\times10^{-4}\), 0.00102). \(E_M\) is 0.0285 (0.0287, 0.0282) and \(E_R\) is 0.0285 (0.0288, 0.0283), level with its \(E(5)\) of 0.0285 (0.0287, 0.0282). Against Model D, which had no group augmentation, \(C_M\) was \(9.81\times10^{-4}\) and \(C_R\) was 0.00181. The extra group data shrank the gap. A gap of size \(10^{-3}\) is still a visible disagreement between \(f(T(X))\) and \(T(f(X))\).

On accuracy, the wrapper does not give up the forecast the underlying network already had. Wrapped B’s \(E(5)\), \(E_M\), and \(E_R\) are all 0.0218, against learned B’s 0.0223, 0.0224, and 0.0223. Wrapped D+B’s three numbers are 0.0281 (0.0284, 0.0279), against learned D+B’s 0.0285. Speed is the same story. Learned D+B’s \(E(2)\) is 0.0261, 0.029, and 0.0313 at 1×, 2×, and 3×. Wrapped D+B’s is 0.0259 (0.0261, 0.0257), 0.0286 (0.0288, 0.0285), and 0.0309 (0.0311, 0.0306). Wrapped B stays with learned B on speed, because the wrapper does not add speed training or reach: \(E(2)\) is 0.0181, 0.0438, and 0.0495, next to 0.0186, 0.0446, and 0.0506.

The cost is at inference. One evaluation of \(\bar f\) runs the underlying network eight times. Learned B takes 0.464 s on the 1,000 clips (0.517, 0.412). Wrapped B takes 3.81 s (4.42, 3.20), 8.2 times as long. Learned D takes 0.720 s (0.725, 0.715). Wrapped D takes 5.24 s (5.20, 5.28), 7.3 times as long. Learned D+B takes 0.712 s (0.718, 0.705). Wrapped D+B takes 4.95 s (4.90, 5.00), 7.0 times as long. Training time is a different clock and was not rerun. D+B stays at 20.72 s per epoch (20.67, 20.78), copied from `results/table5_db.json`.

Averaging the eight restored forecasts blurs the frame when those eight put the ink in different places. The average then holds a faint digit at each of those places, and the squared error against a sharp future goes up. The measured spread says that did not happen on these checkpoints. For wrapped D+B, the mean squared deviation of the eight restored forecasts from their average, over the same 1,000 clips, is \(3.91\times10^{-4}\) (\(3.67\times10^{-4}\), \(4.14\times10^{-4}\)). The worst video of either seed is \(1.13\times10^{-3}\). Wrapped D+B’s \(E(5)\) is 0.0281 against learned D+B’s 0.0285, and wrapped B’s is 0.0218 against learned B’s 0.0223. Both wrapped errors are slightly lower. The eight forecasts already agreed closely enough that replacing \(f\) by \(\bar f\) did not smear the test score.

## Q2.16

The video in Q2.7 is Figure 3, mirror rank 1, dataset 9347, seed 0. Figure 6a uses that same video. The GIF is `figures/gifs/figure6a_cm_rank1.gif`.

Q2.7 had two parts. Row 3, \(f(M(X))\), still had two digits at horizon 5, with the second digit too far down and not far enough left. Row 4, \(M(f(X))\), was one blob, area 913, centred at (32.7, 28.1), because the original forecast had already become one component and a mirror cannot split a component. The rows disagreed, and the per-video \(C_M\) was 0.00624.

The final model removes the disagreement. It does not get horizon 5 right.

On seed-0 symmetrised D+B, row 3 and row 4 of this panel are the same image at every horizon. The per-video gap is \(C_M = 7.987980\times10^{-17}\). Horizons 1 and 2 still have two components. From horizon 3 there is one. At horizon 5 that component has area 1119, peak 0.434, and centroid (32.64, 29.43). The mirrored truth still has two digits, areas 177 and 151, at (42.75, 20.51) and (21.66, 36.57): 2 rows down and 6 columns left, and 5 rows down and 10 columns left, as in Q2.7. The forecast centroid lies between those two digits. It is the same kind of blob Q2.7 reported for row 4, and both rows now show it. This panel’s transformed error is 0.0340, above the seed’s test-set \(E(5)\) of 0.0284.

The symmetriser is the change that made the rows agree. \(\bar f(M(X)) = M(\bar f(X))\) holds for any weights, and the gap on this video is the float residual. Learned symmetry left a real gap: unwrapped D+B has test-set \(C_M = 7.53\times10^{-4}\), and unwrapped B has 0.00106. The missing digit at horizon 5 is still missing. The wrapped forecast has already merged by horizon 3 on the original clip, and exact symmetry copies that merge into both orders. Group augmentation and dilation 2 did not keep the two digits apart through horizon 5, and the average has nothing to split once \(\bar f(X)\) is one component.

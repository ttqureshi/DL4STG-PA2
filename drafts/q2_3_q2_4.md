# Q2.3 and Q2.4 (draft)

## Q2.3

Model A’s reach is 8 original pixels per time step.

The measurement is centre-to-centre on the original 64×64 frame. `pixel_unshuffle(4)` folds each 4×4 patch into channels and leaves a 16×16 grid. Neighbouring cells on that grid have centres 4 original pixels apart. The fold does not bring in any cell beyond the patch that is already sitting there, and the 4×4 patch width is not added. `pixel_shuffle` is the same regrouping in reverse, and the 1×1 head mixes channels at one cell. Neither moves information to another cell. The only spatial mixes are the two 3×3 convolutions (stride 1, padding 1). A 3×3 kernel reads a cell and its eight neighbours, so one application moves information by one folded cell, which is 4 original pixels. The gate nonlinearities and the cell update are pointwise, so they do not add distance. At the border the kernel is cut off by the padding; the longest path is the one in the interior.

Inside one step τ there are three paths from the sources the question names to the prediction \(\hat{x}_{\tau+1}\).

The new folded input \(u_\tau\) is concatenated with \(h^{(1)}_{\tau-1}\) and read by layer 1’s 3×3. That produces \(h^{(1)}_\tau\). Layer 2 reads \(h^{(1)}_\tau\), not the previous hidden state, concatenated with \(h^{(2)}_{\tau-1}\), through its own 3×3. The 1×1 convolution and `pixel_shuffle` then paint the frame. Two 3×3 kernels, and nothing else that moves spatially: 2 cells × 4 pixels = 8 original pixels.

\(h^{(1)}_{\tau-1}\) enters that same layer-1 3×3 and then the layer-2 3×3. It also travels 2 cells, so it also reaches 8 original pixels.

\(h^{(2)}_{\tau-1}\) enters only the layer-2 3×3, then the 1×1 head. That is 1 cell, 4 original pixels.

The cell state does not open a longer path. \(c_{\tau-1}\) is multiplied, at the same cell, by the forget gate. Its value does not shift onto a neighbour. The recurrence still matters: the previous hidden states are inputs to the convolutions, so memory can move, but only as far as the convolutions applied in this step. Because layer 2 consumes the new \(h^{(1)}_\tau\), the new frame and layer 1’s previous state are stacked through both kernels. Layer 2’s previous state sees only one kernel.

The longest path is therefore 8 original pixels. One path that attains it is \(u_\tau \rightarrow\) layer-1 3×3 \(\rightarrow h^{(1)}_\tau \rightarrow\) layer-2 3×3 \(\rightarrow \hat{x}_{\tau+1}\).

Model A is ahead of both references at every horizon. Its E(5) is 0.0221, against 0.0426 for Blank and 0.0641 for Copy-last. Seed 1 is a little better than seed 0 at every horizon, and the two seeds stay close: the E(5) gap between them is 8.98×10^{-4}. Copy-last is worse than Blank at every horizon, and the gap widens. Repeating frame 5 leaves ink where the digit has already moved away and misses the place it has moved to. Blank is wrong only where the future digit actually is. Figure 2 shows that the digits do move between frame 5 and frames 6–10, so a copy of the last input cannot land on the later digits.

Model A’s error grows at each horizon, and the growth slows down. The seed-mean sequence is 0.0169, 0.0203, 0.0226, 0.0244, 0.0261. Horizon 5 is 0.0092 above horizon 1, about 1.54 times the one-step error. The steps between horizons are +0.0034, +0.0023, +0.0018, and +0.0017. Blank does not grow: it stays at 0.0426 for the first three horizons and 0.0425 for the last two. Copy-last does grow, from 0.0492 to 0.0732. Model A’s horizon-5 error, 0.0261, is still below Blank’s 0.0425. The forecast is getting worse as the network feeds its own predictions back in, and it has not yet fallen to the level of predicting nothing.

Figure 2 is the seed-0 model on test videos 9883, 9590, and 9449. On 9883 and 9449 the two digits stay apart in every displayed frame. The predictions put a soft mark near each digit and those marks follow the motion, but the stroke is already gone at frame 6 and the marks are more diffuse by frame 10. The rise in MSE_h is that spreading: the mass is near the digit, and less of it sits on the stroke as the horizon increases.

On 9590 the digits meet. Ground-truth frames 6, 7, and 8 are each one connected region, and so are input frames 4 and 5. The ink count stays 444 pixels through frames 1–10, equal to the two separate areas 252 and 192, so the digits touch and cross without one erasing the other. They are apart again in ground-truth frames 9 and 10. The seed-0 forecast does not keep two strokes through that crossing. From frame 6 onward it draws one soft shape for the pair, and that shape is still a single smear at frames 9 and 10, where the ground truth has separated. Overlap is where the blur stops being one blob per digit and becomes one blob for both.

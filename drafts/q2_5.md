# Q2.5

This is the prediction written before the mirror, rotation, and speed tests. It uses only the reach estimate and Figure 1.

Model A will not pass the mirror test. Its one-step reach is 8 original pixels, which is a statement about how far a 3×3 convolution can move information, not about reflection. The filters are ordinary convolutions, with no mirrored copy of the weights. Figure 1(a) is not symmetric under a horizontal flip: reflecting the measured flow sends θ to 180° − θ and moves 13.2% of the 103,050 kept pixels into a different 20° bin. A mirrored digit is also a different glyph. Both the direction balance and the appearance sit outside the 1× training sample, so I expect f(M(X)) to disagree with M(f(X)) and with M(Y).

Model A will not pass the rotation test, and I expect rotation to be worse than mirroring. A 90° anticlockwise turn changes the glyph further than a left-right flip, and on these same pixels it reassigns 20.0% of the kept flow into a different bin, more than the 13.2% moved by the mirror. Nothing in the two-layer ConvLSTM ties a filter to its quarter-turn.

It should break at 3×, and 2× should already be strained for the faster digits. Figure 1(b) puts the median consecutive-frame speed at 3.32 pixels per frame, inside the 8-pixel reach. Twice that median is 6.64, still under 8 but close to it. The 99th percentile at 1× is 4.67 pixels per frame, which becomes about 9.3 at 2× and is already past the reach, so the fast tail should fail at 2× even if a typical digit does not. Three times the median is 9.95 pixels per frame, beyond 8, so a typical digit at 3× cannot be joined to its previous position in one update. That is the speed at which I expect the forecast to fall apart.

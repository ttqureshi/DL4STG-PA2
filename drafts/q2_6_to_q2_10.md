## Q2.6

Blank and Copy-last have C_M = 0 and C_R = 0. Their transformed errors match their ordinary errors: Blank has E_M = E_R = E(5) = 0.0426, and Copy-last has E_M = E_R = E(5) = 0.0641.

C_T = 0 means f(T(X)) and T(f(X)) are the same image. Blank produces that by predicting zero on every input, and zero is unchanged by a mirror or a rotation. Copy-last produces it because the forecast is the last input frame repeated, and a transform that acts on each frame separately sends the last frame of T(X) to T of the last frame of X. Both rules commute with M and with R. Both rules are wrong on the test set. Blank’s E(5) is 0.0426 and Copy-last’s is 0.0641, against Model A’s 0.0221. A consistency gap read on its own would rank both baselines as perfect. E_T is the same forecast scored against T(Y), so the pair says whether the forecast that commutes with T is the transformed future or only a forecast that happens to commute.

## Q2.8

Rotation is harder than mirroring for Model A, on both numbers in Table 3. Mean C_R is 0.00308 and mean C_M is 0.00183. Mean E_R is 0.0238 and mean E_M is 0.0227. Both seeds agree: seed 0 has C_R 0.00315 against C_M 0.00168, and seed 1 has C_R 0.00302 against C_M 0.00198. The seed-0 per-video medians are 0.00303 for C_R and 0.00158 for C_M, so the gap is the body of Figure 4, not only the four tail videos marked there (C_M 0.00624 and 0.00466, C_R 0.00729 and 0.00676).

Q2.2 separates the three edits. Mirror and rotation both change the glyph and the direction histogram, and neither changes speed: the median stays 3.32 pixels/frame. Mirror moves 13.2% of kept flow into a different 20° bin. Rotation moves 20.0%. The ratio of those shares is 20.0/13.2 = 1.52. The ratio of the consistency gaps is 0.00308/0.00183 = 1.68. E_R stays close to the ordinary E(5) of 0.0221, and far below Blank’s 0.0426, so a rotated digit is still a target the network can partly hit. The damage that grows is C_R, the failure of f(R(X)) to match R(f(X)). That is the motion. A quarter turn rearranges direction more than a left-right flip, and Q2.1 notes that 90° splits each 20° bin across two bins rather than sliding it onto one. The appearance change is real, and Q2.2 says a quarter turn changes the glyph further than a mirror, but an unreadable glyph would push E_R toward Blank. Table 3 does not show that.

## Q2.9

Model A’s predictions fall apart at 2×, and 3× is worse. Mean E(2) is 0.0186 at 1×, 0.0448 at 2×, and 0.052 at 3×. Blank stays at 0.0426, 0.0425, and 0.0426. At 2× the forecast is already above Blank, and at 3× it is further above. It remains below Copy-last (0.0661 at 2×, 0.0703 at 3×). E(5) at 2× is 0.049, against Blank’s 0.0425 on that same window. This is earlier than the Q2.5 call, which put the break at 3× because 3 × 3.32 = 9.95 exceeds the 8-pixel reach and 2 × 3.32 = 6.64 does not.

Thirty-five test videos passed, and 9883 has the largest minimum centroid distance among them, 31.19 pixels. Both components keep areas 204 and 134 on every displayed true frame.

At 1× both digits step right. By frame 7 each centroid is 7 pixels right of frame 5, and the prediction’s centroids are 6.7 and 6.4 pixels right. The marks are soft, with areas about 390 and 260 against 204 and 134, and they sit on the digits.

At 2× the truth jumps 7 pixels right from frame 9 to frame 11. The prediction moves 2.8 and 2.3 pixels. By frame 13 the truth is 11 and 14 pixels right of frame 9, and the prediction is 5.8 and 4.7. The digits are left behind.

At 3× the larger digit bounces. From frame 13 to frame 16 it moves 11 pixels left, and by frame 19 it is 22 pixels left of frame 13. The prediction’s column changes by 0.2 pixels at frame 16. The other digit continues 8 pixels right at frame 16; the prediction moves 0.1 pixels. At frame 19 the forecast is one component, area 647. The failure is a stall, and then the two marks fuse.

The 8-pixel reach is the longest path, the new frame through both 3×3 kernels (Q2.3). The previous layer-2 state, which is where the last position sits, goes through one kernel and travels 4 original pixels. A typical 2× step is 6.64 pixels, already past that path. On 9883 the 2× step is 7 pixels and the forecast moves about 2, so the update is not using the full 8 pixels on the digit. The estimate compared the median speed with the longest path. It missed the shorter memory path, and it missed that a cell-to-cell bound does not carry a whole stroke onto a position several pixels away.

## Q2.10

These results do not say which cause it is. Model A was trained at 1× only. A 7-pixel step is rare in the Figure 1 sample (the share faster than 5 pixels/frame is 0.00465), and the same step is past the 4-pixel memory path. The 3× bounce on video 9883, 11 then 22 pixels, is past the 8-pixel bound as well. A stall fits a reach limit, and it also fits a model repeating the small step it saw in training.

Models C and D are the experiment that separates the two, and they have not been trained. C keeps A’s architecture and adds speed augmentation, so the reach stays the same and fast motion is in the training sample. D keeps C’s training and increases the reach. If C removes the 2× and 3× failure, the missing training motion was the cause. If C still fails and D succeeds, the step was beyond the architecture.

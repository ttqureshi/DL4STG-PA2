\subsection{Assumptions}

The brief leaves the history length and the shape of the hour-ahead output unspecified, so A1 and A2 state them.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A1.} History is \(H=36\) bins, six hours, ending at issuance.
\item \textbf{A2.} The output is six levels at each of S1--S4, at $+10$ through $+60$ minutes, so $\hat Y$ has shape $(B,4,6)$.
\item \textbf{A3.} The archive covers at least one year, including wet and dry periods.
\item \textbf{A4.} On a normal issuance the bin that closes at $t$ has arrived.
\item \textbf{A5.} Gauge errors are small beside a rise that matters.
\item \textbf{A6.} Each rain reading goes in the 10-minute bin that contains its timestamp, and a carried bin gets no attention weight.
\item \textbf{A7.} Releases are piecewise constant between effective times, and the latest already-public announcement is used.
\item \textbf{A8.} S1--S4 and T1 share 10-minute boundaries.
\item \textbf{A9.} Water moves downstream, and travel time is not distance divided by a constant speed.
\item \textbf{A10.} The target is level, and each series is standardized by its training mean and standard deviation.
\item \textbf{A11.} R1 and R2 are not assigned to reaches; both are tokens, with gauge-to-station distance as a feature.
\item \textbf{A12.} The unmonitored tributary has no gauge, and the S3$-$S2 residual is an extra history channel, zero on the other series.
\item \textbf{A13.} Future rainfall and future levels are not inputs, and a future release is written only when it was announced by issuance.
\item \textbf{A14.} Training uses the same clock: inputs at $t$ obey A13, and the target is the level at $t+\Delta,\ldots,t+6\Delta$.
\item \textbf{A15.} The loss is mean squared error of the standardized level over stations, leads, and the batch, with missing targets left out.
\item \textbf{A16.} Along-river distance is used among the reservoir, S1--S4, and T1, and gauge-to-station distance is straight-line.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Notation, equations, and objective}

\(\Delta = 10\) minutes. Lag \(\ell = 0,\ldots,H-1\) means time \(t-\ell\Delta\). Lead \(h=1,\ldots,6\) means time \(t+h\Delta\). \(U_{j,i}=1\) when series \(j\) is upstream of series \(i\). Into S1: the release only. Into S2: the release, S1, and T1. Into S3: those plus S2. Into S4: those plus S3. A station is not upstream of itself. \(\phi(d)=-\log(1+d/d_0)\), with \(d_0\) the median static distance. \(\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{dist}}\) are learned scalars.

A level token carries the standardized level, the mask, and the residual channel. Rain carries standardized rain and its mask. A history release carries actual, schedule, and the actual mask. A future release carries the announced value and the known mask. Each group has its own matrix,
\[
e = W_{\mathrm{group}} x + a_i + b_{\mathrm{time}},
\]
and the token list is \(Z^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}\). For block \(n=1,2\),
\[
\mathrm{score}(p,q) = \frac{Q_p\cdot K_q}{\sqrt{d}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{up}} U_{\mathrm{series}(q),\mathrm{series}(p)} + \lambda_{\mathrm{dist}}\phi(d_{q,p}).
\]
A key whose mask is 0 has score \(-\infty\). The block is residual attention plus a residual feed-forward layer of width \(4d\). The query for lead \(h\) at station \(s\) is \(q_{s,h} = W_q[z_{s,0}; e_h; \tau_s]\), where \(\tau_s\) is how many bins since \(s\) last reported. The loss, with \(m_{s,h}(t)=1\) when the target was recorded, is the mean over the batch of
\[
\frac{\sum_{s,h} m_{s,h}(\hat y^{\mathrm{std}}_{s,h}-y^{\mathrm{std}}_{s,h})^{2}}{\sum_{s,h} m_{s,h}}.
\]
One attention block connects every remaining token, so S4 can see the reservoir in one step, and a second block reuses a mix the first has formed. With \(L=294\) and \(d=64\), one block is about \(1.1\times 10^{7}\) multiply-adds, and the ten-minute gap is set by when the gauges arrive. What is fixed is the series list, \(U\), and the rule that an announcement enters only if it is already public. What is learned is every matrix, both blocks, the readout, and the four bias scalars.

\subsection{Operation}

\textbf{Missing observations.} A missing value is set to 0 after standardization and its mask to 0, so that token gets score \(-\infty\).

\textbf{Delayed observations.} A bin that has not arrived by \(t\) is missing, and the readout sees \(\tau_s\), the bins since that station last reported.

\textbf{Announced future releases.} The latest announcement with announcement time at or before \(t\) and effective time in the lead bin has known mask 1, and if none exists the mask is 0.

\textbf{Scheduled versus actual releases.} History bins carry the recorded actual and the announced schedule, and the future channel is only the schedule.

Each issuance is one forward pass of two attention blocks over 294 tokens of width 64 and 24 queries, which fits between the ten-minute bins.

\subsection{Uncertainties and limitations}

The unmonitored tributary can raise S3 and S4 with no trace in S2, T1, the release, or R1 and R2 until that water reaches S3. I use the S3$-$S2 residual and S3's history, and S4 reads those tokens in one attention block.

A changing travel time can put a peak in the wrong 10-minute bin. The model attends over every lag in the \(H=36\) window, with a separate query for each of the six leads, and uses distance as a feature.

A gauge on the unmonitored tributary would be a ninth series and would show that inflow before it reaches S3. Travel times at a low stage and a high stage would check whether \(H=36\) cuts off ordinary waves, and a catchment plus an error bar would fix which gauge belongs to which reach and how the loss weights the instruments. S1--S4 are still forecast without those extras.

\textbf{Q2.12.} Model D's one-step reach is 16 original pixels, against Model A's 8. A \(3\times 3\) kernel with dilation 2 reads offsets \(-2,0,+2\), so one layer moves 2 folded cells, which is 8 original pixels. The new input passes through both layers, and layer 2 reads layer 1's new hidden state, so the longest path is 4 folded cells, \(4\times 4 = 16\). The path is \(u_{\tau}\rightarrow\) layer-1 dilated \(3\times 3\rightarrow h^{(1)}_{\tau}\rightarrow\) layer-2 dilated \(3\times 3\rightarrow\hat x_{\tau+1}\). The \(1\times 1\) head and the shuffles add nothing. The previous state of layer 2 travels only one dilated kernel, which is 8 original pixels.

\subsection{The final model}

Table 5 supports D+B. Model B cut the rotation gap without moving \(E(5)\), and Model D's rotation gap (0.00181) was still larger than its mirror gap. D+B is Model D trained with both the speed draw and one shared group element on each window. Its \(E(5)\) is 0.0285 (0.0287, 0.0282), slightly under D. Its gaps are \(C_M=7.53\times10^{-4}\) and \(C_R=9.89\times10^{-4}\), with \(E_M=E_R=0.0285\). Its \(E(2)\) is 0.0261, 0.0290, and 0.0313 at \(1\times\), \(2\times\), and \(3\times\). The speed gains survive. The final model is that network wrapped as
\[
\bar f(X)=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{g\in G} g^{-1}\big(f(g(X))\big).
\]
For any \(h\in G\), right multiplication by \(h\) permutes \(G\) and \(h\) factors out of the average, so \(\bar f(h(X))=h(\bar f(X))\). Since \(M\) and \(R\) are in \(G\), \(C_M=C_R=0\) exactly. The measured gaps are the floating-point residual.
